Video startup under network limit, initial cap & segment length

How fast iOS video starts playing — and how it climbs to full quality — across a network-limit × startup-cap × segment-length grid. The residency bar shows where the first 60 s went: buffering, then each rung it dwelled on.

How to read a row

TTFF
first frame decoded (s) — not the felt startup.
Video-start
playhead actually moving (s) — timeControlStatus == .playing. The felt startup.
Start rung / Var @60s
rung joined on / rung settled at a minute in (the link's ceiling).
Settle
seconds from video-start to first reaching the settled rung (the climb).
Startup-eff
bitrate-utilization %: delivered bitrate over 60 s (buffering = 0) ÷ achievable rung. An engineering proxy, not a perceptual QoE score.
Shifts
rung changes during the climb, ↑ up / ↓ down (down = non-monotonic).
Residency
% of the first 60 s spent buffering (hatched) then at each rung (dark→bright = low→high bitrate).

Method

iphone-sim, 90 s plays, content insane_newer_p200_h264 (ladder 360p→2160p). Each cell: is.peak_bitrate_mbps (initial cap) + is.segment ride config-on-connect; proxy.shape.rate_mbps is server-side tc. Cells run 4-at-a-time as independent iphone-sim arms across 4 Fleet sims. Metrics from harness query events; residency & startup-eff integrate the rendition time-series over the first 60 s with non-playing time counted as 0.

Reading the metric

Startup-eff is a bitrate-utilization proxy, not a QoE score. Bitrate isn't perception — ladders are logarithmic, VMAF saturates. Rigorous session-quality standards are ITU-T P.1203/P.1204 (MOS) and VMAF; video-start time itself is industry-standard (CTA-2066 / CMCD).

Caveats

n=1 per cell — levels are large enough to be real; exact seconds jitter, confirm at reps ≥ 3. The 0.8 Mbps limit row is omitted (out of operating range).
