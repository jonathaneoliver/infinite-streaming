Video startup under network limit, initial cap & segment length

How fast iOS video actually starts playing — and how quickly it climbs to full quality — across a network-limit × startup-cap × segment-length grid. Time-to-video is what a viewer feels; time-to-first-frame decoded is not.

How to read a row

TTFF
first frame decoded (s) — video_first_frame_time_ms. Not what the viewer perceives.
Video-start
playhead actually moving (s) — video_start_time_ms, timeControlStatus == .playing. The felt startup.
Start rung
rendition (Mbps) the player joins on. High = over-selection.
Variant @60s
the settled rung a minute in — the link's achievable ceiling.
Time-to-settle
seconds to first reach that settled rung (the ABR climb).
Startup-eff
bitrate-utilization %: time-weighted delivered bitrate over the first 60 s, buffering = 0, ÷ the achievable rung. 100% = held the ceiling the whole minute. An engineering proxy, not a perceptual QoE score.

Method

iphone-sim, 90 s plays, content insane_newer_p200_h264. Each cell: is.peak_bitrate_mbps (initial cap) + is.segment ride config-on-connect; proxy.shape.rate_mbps is server-side tc. Cells run 4-at-a-time as independent iphone-sim arms across 4 Fleet sims (~4× throughput, verified identical to serial). Metrics from harness query events.

Reading the metric

Startup-eff is a bitrate-utilization proxy, not a QoE score. Bitrate is not perceptual quality — encode ladders are logarithmic and perceptual quality (VMAF) saturates, so a 5.8 Mbps rung is not literally 17% of a 33.8 rung to a viewer. The rigorous standards for session quality are ITU-T P.1203/P.1204 (parametric MOS) and VMAF (perceptual); vendor scores (Conviva, Mux, Bitmovin) are proprietary. Video-start time itself is an industry-standard metric (CTA-2066 / CMCD).

Caveats

n=1 per cell — levels are large enough to be real; exact seconds jitter, confirm at reps ≥ 3. The 0.8 Mbps limit row is omitted (out of operating range — startup there degrades into never-starts).
